## Explanation choices and full author read-through

The opening asks what a working connection has established and what an access service still has to deliver. The service meaning is made explicit before following the four responsibilities.

The two native review actions and excluded payment action establish a concrete business boundary. Governance supplies the justified requirement. Implementation follows it into separate person/account records, collection, account association, entitlements, a requested profile, the selected approval and target changes.

Administration is introduced through maintaining the conditions that made the tested design work. Support then traces a specific replacement-account privilege failure, supported scoped recovery and the remaining affected operations. Ongoing governance returns the explanation to changed duties and delivered removal.

The distinctions are developed through the connected work, not an opening dictionary of four job titles. A permission table supports the action comparison; one handoff diagram shows work/evidence connections and explicitly avoids implying engine phases. No table/diagram/question/word quota applies.

The author read the complete saved lesson, including middle/end, four worked decisions and warehouse transfer, as a simulated beginner with basic account familiarity. Corrections made during that pass included replacing an early recipient reference before its definition, avoiding an undeveloped assignment reference, removing unnecessary atomic/all-or-nothing terminology, clarifying actual account absence and separating reading account files from permission to view stock records.

The successful integration authority is explicit. Profile deletion dependencies are explained alongside delivered permission retention. No private engine implementation, fixed processing interval, generic cancellation/retry operation or universal connection-test capability is invented.

This is an author simulation, not an independent beginner study or measured confidence/comprehension result. Actual beginner validation remains separate and pending.

## Example conditions and worked reasoning

The invoice permissions Read invoices and Mark reviewed apply within an agreed business area; Release payments is separate and excluded. Invoice review is an illustrative access-profile label, not a vendor default. No employee biography or arbitrary account/object identifier is added.

The successful recipient has an existing identity and one suitable enabled account. Supported matching uses prepared fields with correct same-person values and a unique intended identity. The visible profile is enabled/requestable and self-requested, the selected manager is correct, and the native two-right additions are supported, enabled, connected and authorized for the technical actor. Both additions are accepted and verified. No future start, separate activation, unrelated permission/rule change or competing configured requirement is supplied.

The two rights are ordinary continuously held entitlements, not activation-based access. The example's simple later removal has no independently required overlapping grant. Application permission semantics and business-area restriction are supplied conditions, not inferred from profile names.

The later incident changes the technical account. Its reads work and both additions are rejected before application; native rights are absent. This is a hypothetical connector test scope and privilege finding, not universal product behavior. Recovery supplies no applied right, other correction or pending related operation before the approved scoped privilege correction and supported additions.

The backlog checkpoint retains 12 other failed operations, including three removals. Those counts are example evidence, not product thresholds. The no-account case permits discovering an existing-but-uncollected/unassociated account instead of assuming actual absence. The reviewer case checks authority and execution state without inventing cancellation. The deletion case separates stopping new effects from withdrawing existing excess.

The warehouse transfer supplies reader/supervisor populations, successful account-file collection, unknown write capability, next-week pilot pressure and a required withdrawal deadline. It requires validated support or an acceptable supported manual route, acceptance evidence and usable handover. It does not treat the deadline or pilot date as product timing behavior.
